Measured fixture geometry was supplied as engineering information to support the machining setup and CAD/CAM program development. CMM measurement did not directly control the CNC machine.
SIX-PART FIXTURE / BATCH MANUFACTURING

One fixture → six component locations → one CNC cycle.

The inspection strategy captured the relevant fixture geometry across the component locations so the machining team could work from measured positional relationships.

01 / FIXTURE METROLOGY

Measure locator position, hole locations, feature diameters, angular relationships, orientations and feature-to-feature distances without inventing numerical values.

02 / COORDINATE FRAME

Establish the measured spatial relationship between the fixture's reference geometry and locating features; keep measured geometry distinct from nominal CAD.

03 / ABERLINK OUTPUT

Detailed Aberlink drawings / measurement outputs documented relevant distances, centres, diameters, angles, orientations and feature relationships for the machining team.

04 / MANUFACTURING FEEDBACK

The measured information supported CAM setup and feature-location decisions, reducing uncertainty when transferring the physical fixture into the programming environment.

MEASUREMENT DATA →
MANUFACTURING CONFIDENCE
Accurate fixture measurement supported the six-axis milling team leader in developing the CAD/CAM machining setup. The objective was to improve the likelihood of right-first-time machining by reducing uncertainty in the measured fixture geometry - not by claiming that CMM measurement alone guaranteed first-pass acceptance.
