ENGINEERING CONTEXT
Offline CAD programming separates much of inspection-program development from physical CMM availability. The customer drawing establishes the inspection intent; a CAD model generated in a compatible system such as SolidWorks, AutoCAD or Siemens NX can be transferred through supported exchange formats such as STEP or IGES. In Aberlink 3D, the measurement sequence, features, probe approaches and orientations, travel and clearance strategy can then be developed before the first-off component is available.
This allows programming work to run in parallel with manufacturing, minimising the amount of CMM programming and adjustment required after the first component arrives. The prepared routine is then brought online and verified against the actual workholding, datum setup, probe accessibility, physical clearance, machine/probe behaviour and measurement results.
RIGHT-FIRST-TIME OBJECTIVE
Offline programming supports a right-first-time objective by developing the CMM measurement strategy before physical execution. Path review can reduce the risk of accessibility and movement issues, but it does not replace verification on the actual CMM.
Complex geometry can increase feature count, probe orientations, path complexity and clearance demands; however, datum structure, workholding, probe selection, accessibility and actual first-off variation also influence successful validation.
